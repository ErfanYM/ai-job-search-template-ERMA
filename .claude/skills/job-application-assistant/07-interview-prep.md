# Interview Preparation Guide

STAR examples should be drawn from the candidate's own real projects and internships
(`01-candidate-profile.md`, and any other profile files `/setup` creates). `/interview`
maps them to a specific posting's likely questions and adds company research. Never
coach a claim that is not in those sources.

## STAR Format

Structure answers as: **Situation** (context), **Task** (your responsibility), **Action**
(what you did), **Result** (outcome). Keep to 1-2 minutes. Be specific. End with what was
learned.

## Ready-Made STAR Examples

Build 3-5 of these from the candidate's own profile, one per notable project/internship.
For each: a one-line title tagging what it demonstrates (system design, ownership, data
quality, debugging, etc), then S/T/A/R in 1-2 sentences each, and a "Use for" line listing
the interview-question types it answers. Example skeleton:

### 1. [Project/Internship Name] (what it demonstrates)
**S:** [Situation - the context and the problem.]
**T:** [Task - what you were responsible for.]
**A:** [Action - what you actually did, concretely.]
**R:** [Result - the measurable outcome.]
**Use for:** "a time you...", "how do you...", "a project you're proud of"

## Common Tough Questions

### "Why are you looking / why did you leave your last role?"
> Answer honestly from the candidate's actual employment history and current goals - what
they're looking for next and why, with no negativity about past roles.

### "You don't have [specific skill/tool/years of experience]."
> Acknowledge it plainly. Bridge to the closest thing the candidate has actually done (a
related tool, a similar problem solved a different way). State the concrete learning path.
Never claim the missing thing.

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
> Ground this in the candidate's actual stated target roles and interests from their
profile, not a generic answer.

### "What's your biggest weakness?"
> Pull a real, specific example from the candidate's own experience (from a STAR example
above), not a generic or humble-brag weakness.

### "Why this company specifically?"
> Customize per company using `/interview` Step 2 research. Must reference specific verified facts: a product, a stated strategic priority, the team's actual work, market position. Never generic.

## Questions You Should Ask Interviewers

### About the Role
- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "What would success look like in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"

### About the Team
- "How big is the team, and how do you divide work?"
- "What does the path from idea to production look like here?"
- "How do you onboard and support someone early in their career?"

### About Tech & Growth
- "What's the current stack for [relevant area], and what's changing?"
- "How much of the work is new build vs maintenance right now?"
- "Is there room to grow toward architecture / design decisions?"
- "How does a junior engineer get code review and feedback here?"

### About Culture
- "How would you describe the team culture?"
- "What does professional development look like here?"
- "What's the flexibility on hybrid / remote?"
- "What do people who thrive on this team have in common?"

## Phone/Video Interview Tips
- Have the STAR examples above open
- Water nearby, smile when speaking (changes tone)
- Ask for clarification on vague questions; it's fine to take a few seconds to think
- End with: "Is there anything about my background you'd like me to expand on?"

## After the Application

### Follow-Up Etiquette
- Don't call just to "stand out" post-submission
- Respect a stated timeline; if none given and 2+ weeks pass, a brief status check is fine
- A short follow-up is fine only if there is genuinely new, relevant information

### Thank-You Notes
- On any update (interview invite, rejection, status), send a brief 2-3 sentence thank-you
- Appreciate their time; keep it short

## Roleplay Guidelines
When the user asks for interview practice:
1. Ask which role/company to simulate
2. Warm-up questions first ("Tell me about yourself")
3. Role-specific technical questions
4. 1-2 behavioral questions using the posting's competencies
5. One tough question or curveball
6. After each answer: brief feedback (what worked, what to sharpen) and which STAR example fits best
7. Coach toward the candidate's natural register - direct, concrete, not over-hedged and not a solo-hero pitch (see `02-behavioral-profile.md`)
